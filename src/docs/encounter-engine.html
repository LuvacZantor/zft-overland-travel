Encounter Engine

The Encounter Engine is a system used to generate and define encounters across different environments.

It determines the nature, scale, and context of an encounter by combining environmental factors, severity, creature type, and situational elements.

Encounters produced by this system represent immediate situations that require response, including conflict, interaction, or avoidance.

The Encounter Engine can be used independently or integrated into other systems, such as Overland Travel.

Encounter Definition

An Encounter is defined by the interaction of the following elements:

  • Environment

  • Severity

  • Encounter Template

  • Creature Category

  • Behavior / Intent

  • Situation State

Procedure Checklist

Follow these steps when generating an Encounter:

  1. Determine Environment

  2. Determine Severity

  3. Determine Encounter Template

  4. Determine Creature Category

  5. Determine Behavior / Intent

  6. Establish Situation State

  7. Resolve Encounter

Environment Types

Environment defines the physical and contextual space in which an encounter occurs.

It establishes the boundaries of what can reasonably exist, shaping available creatures, behaviors, and situations.

Environment is the primary constraint on encounter generation.

Environment List

The following Environment Types are used by the Encounter Engine:

  • Roadways

  • Settled Lands

  • Plains

  • Coast

  • Riverlands

  • Forest

  • Hills

  • Swamp

  • Desert

  • Mountains

  • Arctic

  • Blighted Lands

  • Underground

  • Fey-Touched

Environment Function

Environment determines:

  • What creatures or factions are appropriate

  • What natural features or hazards are present

  • What types of encounters are more or less likely

All other encounter elements must be consistent with the selected Environment.

Severity Function

Severity determines how strong the encounter’s primary threats are and how those threats are structured.

It does not determine the encounter’s Template, Creature Category, Behavior / Intent, or Situation State.

Higher Severity encounters generally involve stronger threats, more dangerous enemy structures, or both.

Severity Output

Severity determines two encounter elements:

  • Creature Strength: How strong the primary threats are compared to the party’s Average Party Level.

  • Enemy Structure: How the encounter is built, such as a lone threat, weak group, leader with support, or boss with minions.

Enemy Structure: Describes how threats are organized within the encounter, including lone enemies, groups, leaders with support, or layered hostile forces.

Severity Constraints

Severity must remain consistent with the Environment and overall context.

If a result conflicts with the established setting, it should be adjusted to fit the situation.

Encounter Template

Encounter Template defines the basic situation or structure of the encounter.

It answers what is happening when the party comes into contact with the encountered presence.

The Template provides the narrative framework for the encounter and helps shape Creature Category, Behavior / Intent, and Situation State.

Template Determination

Roll 1d8 to determine the Encounter Template, or choose one that best fits the situation.

d8

3

A location, object, passage, or individual is being guarded, defended, or restricted.

8

An unusual, supernatural, magical, alien, or inexplicable phenomenon is present in the area.

Template Function

The Encounter Template determines the structure and context of the encounter.

It does not determine the encounter’s danger by itself. Severity handles danger, while the Template defines the situation.

The Template influences:

  • Behavior / Intent

  • Situation State

  • appropriate Creature Categories

  • the encounter’s starting context

Template Usage

After determining the Encounter Template, use it to guide the selection of Creature Category, Behavior / Intent, and Situation State.

If a Template result conflicts with the Environment, Severity, or established context, adjust it to fit the situation.

The Template may be chosen instead of rolled if a specific type of encounter is desired.

Template Conflict Rule

If a Template Resolution conflicts with the Environment, Severity, Creature Category, or established story context, keep the Template’s core function but change the details.

The purpose of the Template should remain intact, even if the specific creature, evidence, behavior, or situation must be adjusted.

Example: Predation in Settled Lands might represent criminals stalking travelers instead of a wild beast hunting prey.

Creature Category

Creature Category defines the nature of the entity or entities involved in the encounter.

It identifies what kind of force is present, independent of specific stat blocks or named creatures.

Creature Category works with Environment, Severity, and Encounter Template to determine what form the encounter takes.

Category Function

Creature Category determines:

  • The general nature and origin of the encounter

  • The role the encountered force plays in the selected Template

  • Which Environments are most appropriate for the encounter

  • How the encounter should feel at the table

Category Constraints

Creature Category must remain consistent with:

  • the selected Environment

  • the determined Severity

  • the chosen Encounter Template

If a Creature Category conflicts with these elements, adjust the creature role while preserving the selected Template’s core function.

Example: If Predation is rolled in Settled Lands with Humanoid as the Creature Category, the predator might be a gang, cult, or corrupt guard detail rather than a wild animal.

Behavior / Intent

Behavior / Intent defines how the encountered creatures or forces are acting when the party encounters them.

It does not determine what the creatures are. Creature Category handles that. Behavior / Intent determines their current posture, motive, or reaction.

Behavior Function

Behavior determines:

  • The initial attitude of the entity toward the party

  • How the entity is likely to act before the party intervenes

  • How quickly the encounter may escalate

  • The likely form of interaction, such as conflict, avoidance, negotiation, pursuit, observation, or cooperation

Behavior Constraints

Behavior must remain consistent with:

  • the Encounter Template

  • the Creature Category

  • the selected Environment

  • the overall context

If a Behavior conflicts with these elements, preserve the encounter’s core function and adjust the visible conduct of the entity.

Example: A Hostile Traveler or Caravan might not attack immediately, but may threaten the party, refuse passage, attempt extortion, or lure them into danger.

Situation State

Situation State defines the encounter’s starting condition when the party becomes aware of it.

It determines distance, awareness, timing, and how much opportunity the party has to respond before the encounter fully unfolds.

Situation State Function

Situation State determines:

  • Whether the encounter is aware of the party

  • How close the encounter is when first noticed

  • How much time the party has to react

  • Whether the situation is static, active, or already resolved

Situation State Constraints

Situation State must remain consistent with the Encounter Template, Behavior / Intent, Environment, and established context.

If a result conflicts with the encounter, keep the State’s function but adjust the details.

Example: A Hidden predator with an Immediate Situation State might begin as an ambush, while a Hidden predator with a Distant Situation State might be seen only through tracks, movement, or signs of stalking.

Encounter Resolution

Encounter Resolution determines how an encounter enters play, how the characters may respond, and what consequences follow from their choices.

Resolution does not require a specific outcome. An encounter may end through combat, negotiation, avoidance, investigation, retreat, or another player-declared action.

Resolution Function

Resolution determines:

  • How the encounter is presented to the players

  • What immediate choices are available

  • How the situation changes during play

  • What consequences follow after the encounter ends

The DM uses the generated Encounter elements to frame the situation, then resolves the party’s actions using the normal rules of play.

Resolution Procedure

Follow these steps when resolving an Encounter:

  1. Frame the Situation

  2. Present the Immediate Choice

  3. Determine Pressure or Escalation

  4. Resolve Player Actions

  5. Apply Consequences

Frame the Situation

Describe what the characters perceive first.

The opening description should establish the encounter’s visible danger, opportunity, or uncertainty without revealing hidden information.

  • What do the characters see, hear, smell, or feel?

  • What is happening right now?

  • Who or what appears to be involved?

  • What detail suggests that action may be required?

Present the Immediate Choice

Every encounter should present at least one meaningful decision.

The party may choose to engage, avoid, observe, negotiate, intervene, flee, or attempt another course of action.

Player Approach

Resolution Focus

Fight

Combat, tactics, terrain, and survival

Negotiate

Attitude, leverage, intent, and concessions

Avoid

Stealth, route choice, timing, and risk

Observe

Clues, behavior, weaknesses, and hidden details

Intervene

Rescue, disruption, protection, or redirection

Retreat

Escape, pursuit, losses, and delayed consequences

Pressure and Escalation

Pressure determines whether the encounter changes if the characters delay, fail, or choose not to act.

Not every encounter requires escalation, but encounters with active threats should usually worsen over time.

Pressure Type

Effect

Time

The situation worsens after a delay

Noise

Combat, spells, or disturbance may attract attention

Morale

Creatures may flee, surrender, panic, or call for aid

Environment

Terrain, weather, hazards, or instability become more dangerous

Reinforcement

Additional creatures may arrive if the encounter continues

Resolution Outcomes

When the encounter ends, determine the outcome based on the party’s actions and the state of the situation.

Outcome

Description

Success

The party achieves its goal without significant new complications

Partial Success

The party succeeds, but at a cost or with an unresolved complication

Costly Victory

The party overcomes the encounter but loses resources, time, position, or advantage

Failure

The party does not achieve its goal, and the situation changes against them

Escalation

The encounter leads directly into a worse or more complicated situation

Delayed Consequence

The full result of the encounter becomes apparent later

Apply Consequences

Consequences should follow naturally from the party’s choices and the encounter’s outcome.

Consequences may be immediate, delayed, local, or connected to another system.

  • Characters lose Hit Points, spells, items, time, or other resources

  • NPCs change attitude toward the party

  • Creatures flee, surrender, pursue, regroup, or spread word of the party

  • The party gains clues, treasure, allies, access, or information

  • The Environment becomes safer, more dangerous, or more unstable

  • A future encounter gains Advantage, Disadvantage, a modifier, or a changed Situation State

Resolution Notes

The Encounter Engine creates situations, not predetermined endings.

The DM should allow player choices to determine the final result whenever possible.

If an encounter is bypassed, delayed, or ignored, it may still affect the world later.
