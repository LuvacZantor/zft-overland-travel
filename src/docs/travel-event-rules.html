Prepare

One chosen travel role gains advantage in the next hex.

Regroup

If Momentum is Risky or Deteriorating, move one step toward Neutral.

Discovery

A Discovery reveals a Point of Interest within the hex that the party may investigate.

Procedure

  1. Roll 1d20 on the appropriate terrain Discovery Table

  2. (Optional) Roll 1d6 on the Discovery Modifier Table

  3. Adjust tone based on Region Danger

  4. Describe the discovery

  5. Players choose whether to interact or continue

Discoveries do not delay travel unless players choose deeper engagement.
Discoveries may provide information, resources, or story opportunities.

Terrain Discovery Tables

Discovery results are determined using terrain-specific tables.

Each terrain type has its own Discovery Table that reflects the environment and tone of the region.

*If a terrain does not have a dedicated table, use the closest appropriate terrain.

Settled and Roadway Areas

  • Roadways and Settled Lands do not use Discovery Tables by default.

  • These areas are considered controlled, maintained, or well-traveled.

  • Discoveries in these areas are rare and should be handled manually or replaced with appropriate encounters or roleplay opportunities.

Complications

A Complication represents a problem, hazard, or developing situation that interferes with travel and requires resolution before the journey continues.

Complications create pressure on the expedition without automatically becoming combat.

They arise from the world and terrain rather than player choice, and may consume time, resources, or momentum.

Complication Categories

Every Complication falls into a category that defines the nature of the problem and how it pressures the party.

The category determines:

  • what kind of situation is occurring

  • what kind of responses are appropriate

  • how the Complication interacts with the world

Categories

Category Guidelines

  • The DM chooses the category when the situation is obvious

  • If unclear, the category can be determined randomly

  • Categories describe the type of problem, not the exact situation

  • The same category can appear differently depending on terrain

Threat Flag

Some Complications introduce a developing danger called a Threat.

A Threat represents an active or approaching danger that may escalate into an Encounter if not addressed.

The Threat Flag is not a separate category. It is applied to a Complication when the situation includes something capable of harming the party.

When to Apply the Threat Flag

Apply the Threat Flag when:

  • A creature or group is tracking, watching, or hunting the party

  • A situation is escalating toward hostility or confrontation

  • A supernatural or environmental force actively threatens harm

Common Sources of Threat

The Threat Flag most often arises from:

  • Omen Complications

  • Social Complications involving tension or hostility

  • Magical or Strange Complications that introduce danger

Navigation, Environment, and Resource Complications do not normally create Threat unless the situation clearly introduces an active danger.

What Threat Means

  • The situation is no longer passive

  • The danger has agency, awareness, or momentum

  • The party may be forced into an Encounter if the situation escalates

Threat

Threat represents a developing danger that persists during travel and may escalate into an Encounter if not controlled.

Threat Persistence

Threat persists between hexes until it is reduced to None or resolved by an Encounter.

Only one Threat may be active at a time.

Threat Track

Stage

Description

None

No active danger

Distant

Signs of danger are present

Closing

The danger is approaching or tracking the party

Immediate

The danger is imminent

Creating Threat

Threat is created when:

  • A Complication introduces an active or escalating danger

  • The DM determines a danger exists in the region

  • Travel conditions logically introduce risk

Advancing Threat

Threat advances when:

  • A Threat-bearing Complication results in a Failed Complication Resolution

  • The party draws attention or fails to mitigate danger

  • The DM determines the danger escalates

Reducing Threat

Threat reduces when:

  • The party achieves strong success

  • The party avoids, escapes, or mitigates danger

  • Time or distance reduces pressure

Encounter Trigger

When Threat reaches Immediate, an Encounter begins.

Encounters

An Encounter represents a direct interaction with an active presence within the hex.

Encounters occur when the party comes into contact with creatures, factions, or forces that require immediate response.

Encounters may involve combat, negotiation, evasion, or other forms of conflict.
